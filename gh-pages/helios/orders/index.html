Every night a GitHub Action reads NVDA closing prices from LSEG and Sanji's position book, then writes the next step of an options wheel as Sanji orders: sell a put, sell a covered call, sell the shares, or wait. A 20/50-day moving average decides whether the wheel turns at all.

How the algorithm works
01 / INPUT
Known closes and the book
Pull roughly 150 calendar days of NVDA daily TRDPRC_1 from LSEG. Require 50 valid closes, and require the newest one to be the last NASDAQ session that has closed. Read mkt/positions.json for NVDA shares, short puts, short calls and cash. No file means a flat book.
02 / FILTER
Is the trend up?
The trend is up when the mean of the last 20 closes is above the mean of the last 50. Ties count as down. The filter only says whether to keep selling options; it never picks a strike.
03 / ORDERS
Take the next wheel step
One market order at the next open, chosen from the table below. Strikes and expiries are Sanji tokens resolved at the open, not numbers fixed tonight.

      
BookTrend upTrend down

        
FlatSHT 1 put, 5DTE+, otm:1 (cash-secured put)Nothing

        
Long 100 shares (put was assigned)SHT 1 call, 5DTE+, otm:1 (covered call)SEL the shares

Assignment is not an order. Sanji applies it after the close: a short put that finishes in the money turns into 100 long shares, and a covered call that finishes in the money takes the shares away. The next night the script reads that book and picks the next row. Shares that are not a multiple of 100 are sold first, because a call covers 100. If the book reports cash below 100 times the last close, the put is skipped; Sanji itself does not check buying power.

    

    
Backtest
This tests the trend filter, not the option income. It holds NVDA stock while the filter says the trend is up and holds cash otherwise, using LSEG daily bars since late 2020. A wheel earns premium on top of that and gives up gains above the call strike, which needs historical option prices and is not modeled here. The backtest calls the exact filter the nightly job uses (signal_from_closes), decides at each close, and fills at the next day's open, the way a Sanji market order would. Returns are split at the open, so the overnight gap belongs to the old position and the day's move to the new one. No commissions or slippage. Prices are split-adjusted. What it shows

      
As of the 2026-11-19 run, the rule turned $1 into about $3.89; holding NVDA turned it into about $10.75. The rule was in the market 64% of days and ran at lower volatility (38% vs 51%), but its Sharpe ratio was lower (0.80 vs 1.06). On a stock that rose tenfold, being out a third of the time is expensive: in 2023 the rule made +150% while holding made +246%.

      
It also did not deliver the protection a trend filter promises. It exited in January 2022, about 17% below the November 2021 peak, but then bought two bear-market rallies, in March and August 2022, and sold each about 30% lower. It lost 59% in 2022 against 51% for holding, and its worst drawdown (−65%) was almost the same as holding's (−66%). Where it helped is the recent decline: down 8% in 2026 against 23% for holding, and flat since the exit on 2026-11-02, which is why tonight's order file is empty.

      
Limits: one stock, one path, and NVDA was picked after it had already risen, which flatters any long rule. Seventeen round trips over six years make costs negligible, so they are left out. Read it as evidence about when the filter keeps the wheel running, not as the wheel's return.

When it runs
The workflow is scheduled for 03:00 UTC, which is 10 p.m. New York in winter and 11 p.m. in summer, following the course's nightly refresh. GitHub starts scheduled jobs late when its runners are busy. On this repo the scheduled run has actually started around 11:00 UTC, about 6 a.m. New York, which is still before the 9:30 open.
The script therefore does not trust the calendar date. It asks the NASDAQ calendar for two sessions measured against the current time: the last session that has already closed, whose close must be the newest LSEG bar, and the first session that has not yet opened, which becomes the order's for date. An on-time run at 10 p.m. Monday and a late run at 6 a.m. Tuesday both plan Tuesday from Monday's close. A manual run during market hours ignores the partial bar and plans the following session. If the newest close is missing, the job fails instead of writing orders from stale data.
After the script writes the files, the workflow commits them and starts the Pages deployment, so this page shows the same orders as the repo.

    
Why this design
A plain wheel sells puts in every market. In a falling market that means getting assigned shares on the way down and then writing calls below the cost basis. The moving-average filter stops new puts and exits shares while the trend is down; the backtest above shows when it would have stood aside. The wheel itself is the strategy I backtested on AAPL earlier this semester, and it is what Sanji's assignment rules were written for.
Every option order is a market order with relative tokens. otm:1 and 5DTE+ need only the listed strikes and expiries. Tokens such as delta:30 and limit prices like mid need a chain snapshot at the open, which Robin does not have yet, so Sanji would reject or leave them unresolved. The cost of a market order is selling at the bid instead of the mid.
Sanji has not published the shape of positions.json. The parser accepts the common shapes (a list of positions, a map keyed by conid, negative quantities or a side field for shorts) and stops the job on anything it cannot read, rather than guessing.
The order id contains the session date and the wheel step, so a repeated nightly run for the same session produces the same identifier. Orders use NVDA's IB conid 4815747 and only fields from the Sanji specification. A data fetch or calendar validation failure stops the job before either order file is overwritten.
The root open_orders.json satisfies the assignment's requested location. An identical mkt/open_orders.json supports the path in Sanji's published schema. The workflow commits both files and this page's signal data, then triggers Pages deployment.
Sanji fills orders from the prices the course machine stores, and it stores only the stocks listed in the repo's price_history.json. NVDA (conid 4815747) is listed there with "tracked": true, so the nightly price job collects its bars alongside the strategy's own LSEG pull.
